## The call
I bought Adobe at $209.41. The "SaaS apocalypse" took the stock down 37% this year on fears that AI will replace creative software, but Adobe keeps beating estimates and raising guidance. At 8.5x LTM EBITDA, the market is pricing a moat that has shattered. I think that moat is intact, and the business growing 11% with 48% margins is the evidence.

## Company overview
Adobe sells subscription software: Creative Cloud (Photoshop, Premiere), Document Cloud (Acrobat), and Experience Cloud for marketers. 96% of FY2025's $23.8B revenue was recurring subscriptions, at a 48% adjusted EBITDA margin. It leads creative and document software, with an $82B market cap and little net debt.

## What the market is missing
The consensus is that generative AI commoditizes Adobe's tools. The numbers say otherwise. Revenue has grown 10–11% a year for five straight years, and adjusted EBITDA margins have held between 47.5% and 49%. In Q2 FY2026, revenue rose 13% to a record $6.62B, beat estimates, and Adobe raised full-year guidance. AI-first ARR tripled to over $500M, so AI is adding revenue, not taking it away.

I think the future of AI in creative work is integration, not replacement. Professionals don't want a separate chatbot to make an image; they want AI inside the tools they already use, connected to their files, brand assets and approval workflows. That is where Adobe is putting it: Firefly is built into Photoshop, Express and Acrobat, so every subscriber gets AI features without changing how they work. Adobe owns the workflow, so it gets to decide how AI shows up in it.

The biggest AI labs aren't trying to rebuild Adobe either. Sam Altman has said OpenAI wants to be the core AI subscription but can't build everything, which leaves room for tools creative teams already depend on.

## Why now: catalysts
- **Q3 FY2026 earnings**, expected mid-September 2026: another beat and steady ARR growth would undercut the decline narrative.
- **Adobe MAX**, expected October 2026: AI product launches and monetization updates for Firefly and Express.
- **Q4 FY2026 earnings and FY2027 guidance**, expected mid-December 2026: the first look at next year's growth and margins.

## Valuation
I valued Adobe with a five-year DCF. The [full model](https://cuny907-my.sharepoint.com/:x:/g/personal/mukund_rao43_login_cuny_edu/IQDduXlAc2BVTYgq5hRwm60tAaIDBkUzOo0x1E7i7zkM1tA?e=VmNXcs) is linked in the sources below.

- **Revenue:** FY2026 at the guidance midpoint of $26.55B, then growth slowing to 9%, 8%, 7% and 6%, reaching $35.4B in FY2030.
- **Margins:** adjusted EBITDA margin of 48% in FY2026, easing to 47% by FY2030 to allow for AI competition and the freemium push.
- **Discount rate:** a 9.9% WACC, from a 4.47% risk-free rate, a 1.40 beta and a 4.31% equity risk premium.

**Comps.** On June 15, Adobe traded at 8.5x LTM EBITDA. Its large-cap software peers traded at 11.8x (Intuit), 12.3x (Salesforce), 15.2x (Microsoft) and 33.5x (ServiceNow), and Autodesk, its closest creative-software peer, traded at 18.8x. The peer median was 15.2x, so Adobe sat at a 44% discount despite higher margins than most of the group. The gap is just as wide on earnings: 11.8x LTM P/E against a 23.8x median.

**Exit.** I use an 11x exit multiple on FY2030 adjusted EBITDA. That is below every peer, close to Intuit and Salesforce, the two names hit hardest by the AI selloff, and about 28% below the median. I am not assuming Adobe re-rates back to its peers, only that it stops trading like a business in decline.

That gives an enterprise value of $164.8B and $411 per share, 96% above my entry.

## Risks and what would make me wrong
- **AI disruption is real:** moderate. It would show up as slowing ARR growth and price cuts in Creative Cloud.
- **Freemium transition:** likely. Management is trading near-term individual ARR for a larger Firefly and Express funnel, which could make the next few quarters look weak.
- **Leadership change:** moderate. The CEO plans to step down once a successor is named, and the CFO left in June.
- **I'm wrong if:** Adobe cuts FY2026 guidance, ARR growth falls below 8%, or the stock closes below $175.

## Thesis
The market treated Adobe as an AI casualty, but its moat was never just the software. It is the workflow. Photoshop and PDF are the industry standard formats, and agencies, studios and marketing teams have built years of files, templates and approval processes around them. Creative professionals train on Adobe tools for their entire careers, and large companies buy it through enterprise contracts that plug into their brand and asset systems. Firefly adds to that moat: it is trained on licensed content, so businesses can use it commercially without legal risk, something most standalone AI tools can't promise.

A new AI model can generate an image, but it can't replace the system a creative team runs on. That is why the business kept compounding at double digits with 48% margins through the selloff. At $209 I didn't need Adobe to win the AI race, only to not be destroyed by it. My DCF puts fair value at $411.
