## The call
I bought Six Flags at $6.77. Since the October 12 Goliath accident, the stock has fallen 43%, and the multiple has gone from 7.6x to 7.0x trailing EBITDA. That's a broken-brand reaction. Past consumer crises suggest Six Flags will recover, and I expect the multiple to return to its pre-accident level.

## Company overview
Six Flags, formed by the 2024 Cedar Fair merger, is North America's largest regional park operator. It earns money from tickets, season passes, and in-park spending. FY2025 revenue was $3.10B and Adjusted EBITDA was $792M. Market cap is about $0.7B against $4.88B of net debt.

## What the market is missing
After catastrophic consumer incidents, Samsung (Note 7), J&J (Tylenol), and Merlin (Alton Towers) recovered quickly. Chipotle (E. coli) and Ardent Leisure (Dreamworld) did not. The difference: concentration, repeat incidents, response, and balance sheet. Dreamworld was a large share of Ardent's earnings. Merlin owned 110 attractions, and its shares fell at most 9% when it warned on profits after a comparable crash.

Six Flags passes two tests: Goliath is one park out of dozens, at season's end, and management quickly closed nine similar coasters. UBS notes attendance kept growing after Six Flags' 2013 New Texas Giant accident. And the business was improving: Q2 same-park EBITDA rose 7%, and the pass base grew 6%.

## Why now: catalysts
- **The consumer-crisis recovery pattern**, expected within about 5 weeks (by late November): Samsung hit an all-time high about five weeks after its Note 7 recall. If Six Flags follows the diversified-company path, the discount should close on a similar timeline.
- **Halloween events wrap**, expected October 31, 2026: whether the closed coasters reopen before the season ends.
- **Q3 results**, expected November 5, 2026: the first read on how much October was hurt, and an update on the closed rides.

## Valuation
TTM same-park EBITDA is $801M and net debt is $4.88B (June 28). With 102.3M shares:

- Pre-accident ($11.92): EV $6.10B, or 7.6x
- Entry ($6.77): EV $5.58B, or 7.0x

Target: 7.62x × $801M = $6.10B, minus $4.88B net debt = $1.22B equity, or $11.92 per share (+76%).

Each 0.1x of multiple moves the stock about $0.78.

## Risks and what would make me wrong
- **Repeat incidents:** moderate. Six Flags retired its X2 coaster after brain-injury lawsuits a month before Goliath. Another incident would make this look like Chipotle, not Merlin.
- **Leverage:** moderate. At about 6x net debt to EBITDA, a slow recovery gets expensive (see Jack in the Box, 1993).
- **I'm wrong if:** the stock closes below $5.50 (6.8x), or the closed coasters stay shut through the season.

## Thesis
The market priced a one-park accident as if it had broken the whole company. History says diversified companies that respond fast to a one-off event recover. Six Flags fits that profile, and leverage makes a 0.6x re-rating worth 76% to the equity. I'm buying the discount and selling when the multiple returns to its pre-accident 7.6x, or $11.92.

## Position closed
I sold FUN on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, at $11.88, a 75.5% return from my $6.77 entry and just under my $11.92 target. The multiple was back at its pre-accident level, so the thesis had played out.
