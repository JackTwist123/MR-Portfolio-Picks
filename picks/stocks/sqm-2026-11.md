## The call

I am long SQM at $48.51, with a $53 target and a $38 stop. The stock has fallen by half from its 52-week high, and it trades at a discount to its lithium peers.

If lithium prices stay flat or fall, I don't lose much. If they rebound, I win big. With a commodity as volatile as lithium, that is a bet worth making, and SQM, as the lowest-cost producer, is the best way to make it.

## Company overview

SQM is a Chilean miner and one of the largest lithium producers in the world. It also sells iodine and fertilizers, but lithium drives most of its profit, so the stock trades on the lithium price.

Its main asset is the Salar de Atacama, a salt flat with the highest lithium concentration in the world. SQM pumps up lithium-rich brine and lets the desert sun evaporate it. That is far cheaper than mining hard rock, which makes SQM the lowest-cost producer in the industry.

Q3 2026 was a strong quarter, with profit up about 3x from a year ago. Still, the price SQM gets for its lithium fell 10% from Q2, and management warned Q4 prices could be substantially lower. The stock dropped on that warning.

## What the market is missing

**The stock is priced like a lithium bust, but lithium isn't in one.** SQM trades at about the same multiple it had in 2023, when prices were collapsing. Today's lithium price is still well above the 2024 bottom, and SQM sells about 50% more lithium than it did then.

**The cheapest producer trades cheaper than its peers.** Higher-cost hard-rock miners trade at higher multiples than SQM. In a downturn they are the ones forced to cut output, not SQM.

## Why now: catalysts

- **Lithium hydroxide futures start trading in China on November 26.** Hydroxide is the other main battery-grade lithium product, and SQM sells it. Until now, carbonate was the only lithium chemical with a futures price in China. A live, public hydroxide price makes lithium easier to price and hedge, and shows quickly where demand really is.
- **Chinese inventory data each month.** Chinese lithium inventories are still falling even at today's low prices. If that continues, demand is absorbing the new supply, and prices have room to recover.
- **Full-year results on March 3, 2027.** SQM gives its 2027 volume guidance. More tons at a low price lifts every scenario below.

## Valuation

Lithium is volatile, so the most sensible way to value SQM is on what it earns at different lithium prices.

**Step 1: what SQM keeps from each $1/kg.** Looking at SQM's results since 2022, it keeps about 60 cents of profit for every $1/kg move in the lithium price.

**Step 2: what $1/kg is worth per share.** At SQM's current volumes, every $1/kg move in lithium is worth about $3.80 a share. I start from this year's earnings and adjust only for the lithium price.

**Step 3: three lithium prices.** Each case uses a price the market has already seen, not a forecast. Everything else stays at this year's pace, and every case uses the peer-average multiple.

- **Bear: the 2024 bust comes back ($10.93/kg).** That is SQM's average price in 2024, the bottom of the last cycle. Getting there takes the 2027 oversupply the market is worried about. Even then, SQM stays profitable on every ton, and higher-cost miners would be the ones shutting down. The stock is worth **$43, down 11%**.
- **Base: today's price holds ($13.66/kg).** This is today's China spot price held for all of 2027. Nothing has to improve; prices just have to stop falling. The stock is worth **$53, up 10%**. This is my target, because it needs no rebound.
- **Bull: prices get back to last quarter ($19.60/kg).** That is what SQM got for its lithium only two months ago, well below the 2026 peak. It needs Chinese inventories to keep shrinking and new supply to arrive slower than feared. The stock is worth **$76, up 56%**.

**Why this is worth it.** If lithium goes all the way back to the 2024 bust, I lose about 11%. If it gets back to last quarter's price, without a new peak, I make about 56%. That is roughly $1 risked to make $5.

## How lithium prices move the stock

- **Most of a price change hits profit.** SQM's costs barely move with price, so most of any price change goes straight to earnings.
- **The stock moves first.** SQM's contracts are tied to market price indices, so the prices it reports lag the market. Investors price the stock off today's lithium price, and reported earnings catch up later.
- **The multiple moves against the cycle.** Investors pay less for each dollar of earnings at the peak and more at the bottom. That cushions the bear case.

## Risks and what would make me wrong

**Supply wins in 2027.** S&P Global reports that the market expects lithium to swing into oversupply next year, as new supply comes on and energy-storage demand growth slows. That is the road to my bear case.

**Near-term prints get worse.** Management already warned that Q4 prices will be lower. Because of the price lag, the next quarter or two will look weak even if lithium stabilizes.

**I cannot forecast the lithium price.** This trade is a lithium call. My valuation shows what each price is worth. It does not say which price we get.

**What makes me wrong.** I exit if the stock closes below $38. At that price, the market is pricing lithium below the 2024 bust, which means my bear case is too optimistic. I also exit if the March 3 guidance shows lower 2027 volumes.

## Thesis

SQM is the lowest-cost lithium producer, yet it trades cheaper than its higher-cost peers and is priced like a lithium bust. Valued on lithium prices the market has already seen:
- If prices fall back to the 2024 bust, I lose a little.
- If they hold, I make a little.
- If they rebound to last quarter's level, I make a lot.

With a commodity this volatile, that is a bet worth taking. The $38 stop is where the market would be pricing worse than the last bust.
